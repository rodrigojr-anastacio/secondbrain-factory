Right now, in the middle of Hanoi, an American B-52 bomber is sitting in a pond. It fell there on the night of December 27, 1972, the ninth night of Linebacker II, the biggest B-52 bombing campaign ever flown. Five years ago, Vietnam lifted the wreck out of the water. Then they put it back.

In this video: the eleven minutes a Hanoi missile battalion tracked the bomber; the boy whose house it hit, and the young woman nearby who had been home for nine days; why the plaque at the pond and America's own records don't agree; the name the internet gives the wreck, and why it doesn't fit; a navigator who came back fifty years later to stand at this pond; the day the last American prisoners flew out of Hanoi; and why Vietnam wants the bomber right where it fell.

How we made this video: we wrote it from the U.S. Air Force's own history of Linebacker II, the crews' loss records, Vietnamese reporting from the pond, and the reporting on the former prisoners' 2023 trip back (sources below). The real voices are Jeff Meyer, the historian at Andersen Air Force Base on Guam; retired Lt. Col. Richard Brenneman, an F-4 pilot held in Hanoi; and retired Lt. Gen. John Gonge, who ran the 1973 airlift home. Credits below.
