Right now, a Japanese submarine is lying in the grass on a beach in Alaska. It's 78 feet long, its hull is blown open from the inside, and it's still lying right where it was left in 1943. This is Kiska, near the far end of the Aleutian Islands, where Japanese troops came ashore six months after Pearl Harbor and stayed for more than a year.

In this video: the Navy weatherman who hid in the grass for 50 days, the six midget submarines Japan meant for Midway, the Battle of Attu and the doctor's diary, the night two American battleships fired 518 shells at nothing, the admiral who went fishing while he waited for fog, 34,000 men landing on an empty island, and who really blew this submarine open.

How we made this video: we wrote it from Charles House's oral history, Dr. Paul Nobuo Tatsuguchi's diary, the Army's and Navy's official histories of the Aleutians campaign, the Park Service's records for Kiska and Attu, and NOAA's 2018 survey of Kiska's waters. The 3D cut-away of the Type A submarine, the maps and the graphics were made for this video. Credits below.
