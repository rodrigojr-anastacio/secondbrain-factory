Right now, inside a hill in the middle of Germany, there's a rocket factory: two tunnels, each more than a mile long, with forty-six halls between them like the rungs of a ladder. This is where the Nazis built the V-2, the world's first long-range guided missile, with the forced labor of concentration camp prisoners. About 20,000 of the 60,000 people who passed through the Mittelbau-Dora camps didn't survive. The entrances were blown shut in 1947. Today a short stretch can be visited with a guide, and almost all of it stays closed.

In this video: why the factory went into this hill; what the American soldiers found on April 11, 1945; the nine-day race to ship a hundred rockets' worth of parts out before the Soviets took over; the men who ran the factory and the rockets they built for the Moon; what divers found in the flooded halls; why the hill is still sealed; and the words the memorial itself uses about the tunnel.

The 3D in this video is our reconstruction from Thuringia's 1-meter terrain model and the tunnel plan on OpenStreetMap; the flooded halls are drawn from the memorial's and the divers' own descriptions. Nobody can film the sealed part.
