Right now, under the farm fields of Normandy, there are wrecks of American planes from D-Day. Some hit the ground so hard they went deep into it. For decades, French farmers have plowed right over them, and some of the men who flew them are still missing. Right after the war, Army search teams dug at some of these crash sites, then gave up: on paper, the men became "non-recoverable." Eighty years later, the Americans are back, taking fields apart one square at a time.

In this video: a 23-year-old P-47 pilot's three missions on D-Day; his mother, who lost two Williams to France in two different wars; the propeller a French village left in a field to mark the spot; the C-47 shot down over the Cotentin before dawn on D-Day, with three men never found; how a 2024 dig works, bucket by bucket; the lab that matches a tooth to a family; the new DNA test that reaches further out on the family tree; the twins reunited in Normandy; what the bronze rosette on the Walls of the Missing means; and the real reason the searches keep starting in Normandy.

How we made this video: we wrote it from the Defense POW/MIA Accounting Agency's recovery missions in France (2017-2025), the American Battle Monuments Commission, the U.S. Army Air Forces' Missing Air Crew Report 7651, the 366th Fighter Wing's film of 2nd Lt. William J. McGowan's burial (July 9, 2022), and the reporting of the Minneapolis Star Tribune, the Bozeman Daily Chronicle, CNN and Stars and Stripes. The real voices are their own: a U.S. soldier on DPAA's 2018 dig in France; Christine Reilly, the 366th Fighter Wing's historian; Capt. Gene Harb, a DPAA recovery team leader, near Calais; the 101st Airborne Division's remarks at the C-47 Memorial Garden in Picauville (2026). The Taps you hear is the Taps played at Bill's burial. The aerial photographs of Moon-sur-Elle are IGN's. The maps and the 3D field were made for this video (labelled MODEL on screen). Credits below.

IF SOMEONE IN YOUR FAMILY IS STILL MISSING
A family DNA sample (a cheek swab) is how the lab names them. Start here:
Defense POW/MIA Accounting Agency, for families: https://www.dpaa.mil/Families/
Who to call (each service's casualty office collects the sample): https://www.dpaa.mil/Families/Contact-Information/
