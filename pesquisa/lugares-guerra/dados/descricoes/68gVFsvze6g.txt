Right now, under the meadows of Bavaria, in southern Germany, there are wrecks of American bombers. One of them, a B-17 called Shoo-Shoo Baby II, came down on July 18, 1944, with its bombs still on board, and left a crater a hundred feet across. About 2,100 Americans are still missing in Germany, and American teams still come back to dig. Yet America looks after twenty-six military cemeteries overseas, and not one of them is in Germany.

In this video: the thirty-five seconds that wiped out the last box of the formation over the Allgäu; a dairy farmer's son from Metropolis, Illinois (yes, that Metropolis), who may have died trying to save another man on board; a German farmer's 1944 bill for bringing a dead American airman down from a tree; the wallet marked F.W. found behind the Iron Curtain; the village archivist who tracked down all sixty-three crash sites; the DNA that passes only through mothers; a funeral held eighty years to the day after the crash; why there isn't a single American war cemetery in Germany; and the two men from Shoo-Shoo Baby II who are still missing.

How we made this video: we wrote it from the Defense POW/MIA Accounting Agency's announcements for William "Luster" Leukering, Edgar Mills and Francis Wiemerslage, the American Battle Monuments Commission's records, the U.S. Army's own history of its war dead (Steere and Boardman, 1957), Leukering's obituary (with war correspondent Pat Frank's 1945 report), WKMS's reporting from his funeral, and the Schwäbische Zeitung's reporting from the Allgäu. The real voice in the video is A1C Joshua Clunan's, at the 2025 dig near Ramstein. Credits below.
