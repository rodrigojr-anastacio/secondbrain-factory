Right now, inside a mountain in Colorado, there's a city that's been waiting sixty years for World War Three. Fifteen steel buildings stand on giant steel springs, under almost two thousand feet of granite, behind two blast doors that weigh twenty-five tons each. Hundreds of people work inside it every day. And you're not allowed in.

In this video, we follow the chain of reasons that keeps you out: the newspaper typo that turned the men who watch for nuclear missiles into Santa trackers, the night in 1979 when the mountain's own computers told America a Soviet attack had begun, the 46-cent chip that did it again seven months later, the movie that made a president ask "Could something like this really happen?", the morning the blast doors swung shut on September 11th, and why, after the military moved out of the mountain, it moved back in.

How we made this video: we wrote it from declassified records on the 1979 and 1980 false alarms (the National Security Archive), a GAO report, and Space Force and Defense Department records. The 3D model of the mountain and the complex inside it was built for this video from public dimensions and USGS elevation data, and the maps and the reconstructed warning displays were drawn for it. The archive film and photos are credited below.
