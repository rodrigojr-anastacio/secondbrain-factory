The famous pictures of Lincoln's death show a crowd around the bed, his wife at his side and Andrew Johnson among the mourners. Neither was in the room, and almost every one of those pictures was made by somebody who wasn't there. The real photographs from those weeks are few: an empty bed photographed by two brothers who boarded upstairs, a coffin photographed in New York City Hall that the Secretary of War ordered destroyed, Booth's body photographed on a warship and never seen again, and a hanging photographed plate by plate and sold in a Washington bookshop. This is what the record says happened to each of them.
